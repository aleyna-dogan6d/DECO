    How comments will work

    • We do not write comments in farmers' names. The example cards above are clearly marked as examples; they are not real comments.
    • Identity is not verified. A comment is one farmer's personal experience; it is not advice. Your field may be different.
    • Only a first name and the district are shown, never a surname, phone number, e-mail or the location of a field.
    • Comments are shown in the language they were written in.

    About DECO

    DECO: DEcision support for CrOp rotation. It helps a farmer compare the rotation they grow today with up to three alternatives, using NASA data for their area.

    How to use it

    1. Choose your field: on the "Plan my field" page, search for a place, press "Use my location" or click on the map.
    2. Look at the past: pick a NASA layer and press "Play the past". The field card shows a 2001–2024 rain and temperature summary for your area.
    3. Describe your field: press "Create a plan for this field" and enter your current rotation (crop, sowing month, harvest month), soil, water, slope, your priorities and the crops you can sell easily.
    4. Compare rotations: your own rotation next to up to three alternatives. "Look into this option" shows how the scores came out and a transition calendar.
    5. Move the "What if the climate changes?" sliders; if you like, save your field on the "My field" page and keep a monthly note. Talk to a local agricultural adviser before you decide.

    The challenge

    The challenge asks for a tool that compares at least three crop rotations, including the one the farmer uses now, and shows for each why it is suggested, the data behind it, its benefits, what is not known, and its risks.

    How it works, honestly

    • The comparison is rule-based. It is not artificial intelligence, not a forecast and not a crop growth model.
    • Climate comes from NASA POWER (2001–2024 monthly averages); crop needs come from FAO ECOCROP.
    • Each rotation gets six scores from 1 to 5: climate fit, water, nitrogen, soil cover, pest cycle and sellability. Your priorities set the weights.
    • Alternatives change one thing in your rotation: they fill an empty period or replace one crop.
    • Some thresholds were chosen by the team and were tried in only six places. The site marks them as such.
    • Satellite and climate data describe the region around a field, not the single field. Talk to a local adviser before changing what you plant.

    Future plans

    These are plans; none of them is done yet.

    • Real accounts, so that a profile can be opened from other devices (for now the profile is a prototype that lives only in the browser).
    • Improving the rules with farmers' notes and comments.
    • Training modules: short, plain explanations of crop rotation and soil health.
    • Taking the slope automatically from SRTM and the soil type from SoilGrids (we have not tried these two sources yet).
    • More crops.
    • Sowing-time alerts based on short-term weather forecasts (Meteomatics).
    • Our own domain name.
